      How this works, in plain English

      A facultative reinsurance desk receives a cash call from a cedent. The question is always the same: is this number right? This cockpit answers it with a proof, not an opinion.

      1 · A chatbot alone cannot be trusted with this

      Ask a language model to add up a claim and it may invent a number. Ask it to read an ALAE clause and it may pick the wrong one. Both errors are fluent, confident, and expensive. The numbers here never come from a language model: they come from a compiled Lean 4 program, where a wrong answer fails to compile.

      2 · Agentic means a pipeline, not a paragraph

      Four steps run in order: read the slip and the cash call; verify every figure against the certificate; post a double-entry journal; draft the broker memo. Each step consumes the previous step's structured output, so a number cannot be invented between steps — it is carried.

      3 · Lean 4 is the arithmetic

      Money is an exact integer number of US cents. There is no floating point anywhere, so there is no rounding drift. The kernel proves that debits equal credits (facJournal_balanced) and that no clause can pay more than ground-up indemnity plus ALAE (fac_no_overrecovery).

      4 · The clauses are the product

      ALAE inside the limit consumes the layer. ALAE pro-rata in addition pays on top, but only in proportion to ceded indemnity — so it cannot pierce an attachment on its own. Prior paid is cumulative: the incremental due is the cumulative liability less everything already settled.

      The three verdicts you will see

      • APPROVED_EXACT — the cash call equals the certificate arithmetic to the dollar.
      • DISPUTE_OVERBILLED — the cedent billed more than the certificate supports; the memo states the exact adjustment.
      • REVIEW_UNDERBILLED — the cedent billed less than the certificate supports; usually a timing or allocation question, not an error.
